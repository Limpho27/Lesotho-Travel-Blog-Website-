The Founding of Thaba Bosiu

Thaba Bosiu became the mountain fortress of King Moshoeshoe I in the early 1800s. The name “Thaba Bosiu” means “Mountain at Night” because the mountain was believed to grow larger after sunset, making it difficult for enemies to attack.

During times of conflict and invasion, Moshoeshoe I used the mountain as a place of safety for the Basotho people. The natural cliffs and steep sides protected thousands of people from enemy attacks and helped preserve Basotho culture and traditions.

Today, Thaba Bosiu remains one of Lesotho’s most important historical landmarks and is visited by tourists, historians, and students who want to learn more about the origins of the Basotho nation.

The Basotho Kingdom

The Basotho Kingdom was formed under the leadership of King Moshoeshoe I, who united different clans and communities into one strong nation during a difficult period in Southern African history.

Moshoeshoe I became respected for his intelligence, peaceful diplomacy, and ability to negotiate with neighboring tribes and European settlers. Instead of relying only on war, he often used dialogue and cooperation to protect his people.

The kingdom grew stronger through unity, cultural identity, and shared traditions. Even today, Moshoeshoe I is remembered as the founder of the Basotho nation and one of Africa’s greatest leaders.

Lesotho’s Independence

Lesotho gained independence from British colonial rule on 4 October 1966 and officially became a sovereign nation. Before independence, the country was known as Basutoland.

Independence was an important milestone because it allowed the people of Lesotho to govern themselves, protect their cultural identity, and build their own future as a nation.

Every year, Independence Day is celebrated with national events, traditional dances, music, military parades, and speeches that honor the country’s history, freedom, and achievements.
